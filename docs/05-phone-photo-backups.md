# Phone Photo Backups

This is the easiest chapter in the book and the one with the highest
emotional return, because for most humans the phone camera *is* the family
archive. If your photos exist in exactly one place — a pocket-sized glass
rectangle that has survived bathtubs, toilets, and fed-up toddlers — this
chapter is for you.

## The one-tap win: automatic upload

Both big phone ecosystems can back up photos automatically over Wi-Fi. Set
it once, forever reap the benefits. Wi-Fi-only and "while charging" settings
keep this invisible to your battery and data plan.

### Android (Google Photos)

1. Open the **Google Photos** app.
2. Tap your profile picture (top right) → **Photos settings** → **Back up & sync**.
3. Turn **Back up** on. Make sure it says Wi-Fi only.
4. Leave the phone on and charging one evening; the first full upload can
   take a while. After that it's trickle, not flood.

### iPhone (iCloud Photos)

1. Open **Settings** → your name at top → **iCloud** → **Photos**.
2. Turn on **Sync this iPhone** (or **iCloud Photos** on older iOS).
3. Be aware: iCloud's free tier is small (5 GB). A photo-heavy phone
   will fill it, and then iCloud stops uploading (with a gentle nag). Check
   Settings → iCloud → storage now and then.

### Then verify (this is the important part)

Open the same photo service **on a computer** — browser, photos.google.com
or icloud.com — once, today. Seeing your photos sitting on a screen bigger
than your phone is the moment this chapter clicks. It's also your first
restore drill in disguise.

## The cable-and-computer alternative (and its friends)

If you don't want your photos with a phone-company-adjacent cloud, or the
free tier doesn't fit, here's the offline pattern — plain, dumb, and it
works:

**Monthly (or whenever you remember):**

1. Plug the phone into the computer with its charging cable.
2. Phone may ask "Allow file transfer?" — say yes/"trust this computer."
3. Copy the phone's photo folder to the computer, into a dated folder:
   - **Android:** phone shows up as a drive — copy `DCIM`.
   - **iPhone:** use the Photos app's Import feature (or Image Capture on
     a Mac / the auto-import dialog on Windows).
4. Rename the folder `photos-2026-10` etc. Dated folders are price-of-entry
   for history you can actually browse in 2036.

Then the weekly external-drive routine *already* copies that new folder
onto your drive if photos live inside your treasure folder. Two habits,
one chain: **phone → computer → drive → (relative's house)**.

Third path worth knowing: some photo services (and phone makers) have a
"transfer to new phone" tool. Use it, but only as the *convenience* copy —
it's a migration tool, not a backup history you can browse from a decade
away.

## Into new-phone hands

Every two-ish years you'll get a new phone. The move is where photos die,
so make it boring:

1. Old phone: confirm backup is **complete** (see the check below).
2. Do the official transfer the vendor offers — that's the smooth one.
3. **Keep the old phone for two weeks before wiping.** This is the calmest
   of all cheap insurance: nothing gets lost in transition.
4. After the wipe issues, check the new phone's photo count against the
   count the backup app shows.
5. Then, and only then, factory-reset the old one.

## A five-minute audit for the photo anxious

Answer these; if all yes, you're objectively safer than the average
smartphone owner:

- [ ] Does backup say "on" and "complete" (no permanent spinner)?
- [ ] Have you *seen* your photos from a different device (computer
      browser or tablet)?
- [ ] Is there also a physical copy — drive, computer folder, or printed favorites?
- [ ] Do you know the account email and password — and is the password
      manager's emergency sheet saved where a trusted person could get at it?

> **Passwords note:** never write your actual password on the worksheet.
> Keep it in a password manager, and keep a *recovery method* (the
> emergency kit that manager generates) somewhere safe. The worksheet
> should just say "photos live in Google Photos / account email
> grandma-photo-1987@gmail.com" so a helper could find the trail.

## Do this today

1. Turn on automatic backup for photos (Android or iPhone section above).
2. Do the verify step: see your photos from a computer before the day ends.
3. If the free tier doesn't fit or you're privacy-hesitant, plug the phone
   into the computer and do one manual monthly copy. Date it. Label it.
