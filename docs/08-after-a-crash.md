# After a Crash: The First Hour

Something went wrong. The laptop won't start, the drive is clicking, the
phone went through the washing machine, or the little "file not found"
arrow just pointed at a folder that was never really empty in your heart.
Pause. Breathe. This chapter is the calm friend telling you what actually
matters, in order.

## The first rule of crash club

**Do nothing impulsive.** No factory resets, no "repair" prompts you don't
understand, no installing recovery software onto the sick drive. The files
that look lost are usually *recoverable* — right up until a well-meaning
click makes them not. Everything from here is slow and deliberate.

## Step 0 — Figure out what kind of bad this is

Mostly this means answering one question: **what is actually gone?**

- Device won't start → data is probably still fine on the drive.
- Drive makes clicking/grinding noises → **stop powering it.** Power off,
  leave it off. (No freeze-it-in-rice folklore; that's not a thing that
  helps. There's no home fix for physical drive damage.)
- Files deleted by accident → see step 3's software-free options first;
  check trash/Recycle Bin before anything fancier.
- Phone drowned → don't charge it and don't press buttons; dry it, then
  see a repair shop quickly. Data-out-of-dead-phones is a real repair
  shop specialty.
- Ransomware or a scary "all files encrypted" message → stop using the
  machine, don't pay messaging strangers, get help from a real person
  before anything else.

## Step 1 — Take stock with the worksheet

Grab your backup list (this is why the worksheet exists):

- What are the treasures on the affected device? (Your list, circa "Do
  this today," chapter 1.)
- Which of them have copies elsewhere — drive? cloud? phone?
- Anything whose *only* copy lived on the sick device? Circle it in pen.
  That circle is your recovery priority list, and it's usually shorter
  and more survivable than panic says.

## Step 2 — Check what's already safe elsewhere

Before any rescuing, count what's *already* protected by your 3-2-1 work:

- Open the cloud folder on another device. What's there?
- Plug in the external drive (to a **healthy** computer, not the sick
  one) and browse your dated folders.
- For strange phone cases: check the auto-backup app's website.

Good news moment: it's very common that 90% of everything worrying you is
already fine. Realize that first. Calm is a strategy.

## Step 3 — Recover the still-lost pieces

Work the places where deleted/lost things get better politely:

1. **Trash/Recycle Bin** on the device — obvious, but at 1 a.m. it's not.
2. **Cloud version history** — most sync services keep previous versions
   and a trash folder for roughly 30 days; also check "deleted files" in
   the app.
3. **Email drafts/attachments** — documents sent to anyone live in Sent
   and in the recipient's inbox. Ask them to forward it (everyone's been
   there; nobody minds).
4. Phone auto-backup timelines, "recently deleted" in photo apps (usually
   30 days).

If steps 1–4 recover it: log the recovery in your worksheet and skip to
step 4. Deep software-recovery tools (the ones that "scan the drive") are
**last-resort** and can make things worse if used by hopeful beginners on
the same device that's still sick. If the circle-list items can't be fixed
with steps 1–4, an experienced local shop is a real, honest option — bring
them the drive, let them say what's possible. Data-recovery folks charge
because the work is genuinely hard, but the genuinely-important-files
version is usually cheaper than the grief.

## Step 4 — Only now, fix the device

Reinstall, repair, update, "reset," whatever the tech fate called for.
You're doing this *after* the data decision, never before. And when the
device comes back:

- Restore from backup/first-setup tools rather than manually copying, when
  your operating system offers it — it's more thorough than drag-and-drop.
- Re-protect: sign in, re-enable automatic backups, sync the drive copy.

## Step 5 — The post-mortem (two lines, no blame)

Answer on your worksheet:

- **What survived, and why?** (Which copy saved you?)
- **What gap did this expose?** (The missing offsite copy. The photo
  backup that was off. The password that was only in your head.)

Then — this week, while it's fresh — close that gap. Recovering from a
crash *and* updating your setup is the whole system working. A crash you
survived without losing sleep is the whole system *winning*.

## The silver lining, friend-final

There's a secret at the bottom of every crash story: the painful part is
almost never the hardware. It's "I was going to set up backups." You're
reading the chapter that means you weren't going to say that. The boring
copies in the drawer, the cloud tremor you set on a Tuesday, the drill you
ran — one of them is about to make this the most boring catastrophe story
ever told at dinner.

You did the boring thing. It saved you. Go update the tape on the drive,
pour something nice, and log the win on the worksheet.
