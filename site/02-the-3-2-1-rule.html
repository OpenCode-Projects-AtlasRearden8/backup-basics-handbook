The 3-2-1 Rule, No Jargon

Every serious backup guide eventually says "3-2-1" and then buries it in storage-engineer language. Here's the friendly version.

The rule

Keep:

  • 3 copies of anything you care about,
  • on 2 different kinds of thing,
  • with 1 copy somewhere else entirely.

That's it. Now let's make sure each number makes sense, because the logic is what makes the rule stick.

Why 3 copies?

Because stuff happens twice, at the same time, more often than you'd think. Your laptop dies while your external drive is unplugged and useless. The cloud account won't sync while your house's internet is down. One backup is a spare. Two backups are a system.

The original copy counts as number one. So "3 copies" usually means: original + one drive + one cloud account.

Why 2 different kinds?

Because two copies on the same "kind" of thing tend to die together. Two folders on the same laptop = one coffee spill. Two drives from the same delivery box that sit next to each other = one lightning strike, one theft, one surge. Different kinds might be:

  • the original on your computer, plus
  • an external drive, plus
  • an online account (cloud).

The "different kind" idea is really about not putting eggs in one basket shape. A USB stick and an external drive both count as drives, but they're different physical objects and that already helps.

Why 1 somewhere else?

Because some disasters eat everything in the room: fire, flood, theft, the notorious lightning strike. If your spare drive lives in the same drawer as the laptop, a burglar gets a two-for-one.

"Somewhere else" can be low-tech and free-ish: a relative's closet, your desk drawer at work, a fireproof box in the garage, or — easiest of all — a cloud account, which is "somewhere else" by definition.

What counts, what doesn't

| This... | Counts as a 3-2-1 copy? | | --- | --- | | Original file on your laptop | Yes — it's copy #1 | | Copy on an external drive | Yes | | Copy in a cloud account | Yes | | Copy on the same laptop, "in a different folder" | No — one basket | | Copy on a drive that lives in the same bag as the laptop | Half — better than nothing, dies in the fire | | Two cloud accounts | Yes — genuinely independent | | A sync folder (file deleted here = deleted there) | It's a mirror, not a history — see the warning box |

Friendly warning: a syncing folder keeps the latest version, both ways. That's great for convenience and terrible for "I deleted it last Tuesday and want it back." That's why at least one of your copies should be a plain, dumb copy — a drive you plug in and copy to — that doesn't know or care what you deleted elsewhere.

A real-life example

Meet Pat. Pat's treasures: 60 GB of family photos, tax PDFs, a resume, and the spreadsheet that runs the household. Pat's 3-2-1:

  • Copy 1 — lives on the laptop (original).
  • Copy 2 — an external drive, plugged in Sunday nights, one folder

copied over. Label says "PHOTOS+PAPERS."

  • Copy 3 — phone photos auto-upload to a free cloud tier; Pat also drops

the Documents folder into a free cloud drive once a month.

Total cost: one drive, zero dollars of software, ten minutes a week. Pat is not a genius. Pat just started.

Cheat-sheet version

Three copies. Two kinds. One elsewhere. Test it. (The "test it" part is chapter 7 — restore drills — and it's the half everyone skips. Not you, though.)

Do this today

  1. Look at yesterday's top-three folder.
  2. Decide its home #2 and home #3: an external drive (chapter 3 has buying

advice) and either a cloud account or a second drive that lives elsewhere.

  1. Write those two homes on your worksheet. That's your 3-2-1 plan — the

actual copying is just recipes waiting to be run.
