Free Cloud vs. Local, Honestly

The friendly truth: this isn't a battle. Cloud and local drives are good at completely different disasters, and the 3-2-1 rule already told you to use both. This chapter just makes the trade-offs honest so you can pick each one's job without marketing language clouding your view. (Sorry. Couldn't resist.)

What "the cloud" means here

Somebody else's computer that you reach over the internet, run by a big company, with an app that quietly copies your files up to it. Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, iCloud — same shape, slightly different prices and limits.

What each is good at

Cloud is best at:

  • Offsite automatic — the "1 somewhere else" copies itself while you

sleep. Theft, fire, apartment flood: your files are in a data center in another state, sipping coffee, unbothered.

  • Sync — files follow you between phone and laptop with no cables.
  • Sharing — send a photo to grandma without a single attachment.
  • Version history, on most services — handy for "I overwrote my résumé

and want Tuesday's back."

Local (your own drive) is best at:

  • Speed — a whole folder moves in minutes, not upload-minutes. Huge

video libraries and big archives are much happier on a drive.

  • Privacy — no company holds the keys, no account, no terms of service

to click through.

  • No internet needed — works during the storm, works on the train.
  • No monthly bill, ever — one purchase and it's yours.
  • It can't be locked out — a company can suspend your account and take

your access with it (rare! but it happens). Nobody can suspend your external drive.

The real weaknesses

Every tool fails differently, and knowing how a thing fails tells you what to guard against.

| | Cloud (free tier) | Local drive | | --- | --- | --- | | Fails when... | Account issues, tier changes, forgotten passwords, internet down | Drive breaks, is lost, coffee, the laptop falls | | Fees | Free up to a limit (as of writing: iCloud 5 GB, Google Drive 15 GB, OneDrive 5 GB, Dropbox ~2 GB) | One purchase | | Privacy | The provider can read your files (metadata at minimum); use strong account security | Totally yours | | Speed | Upload bandwidth-limited | Recipe-card fast | | Offsite | Built in | Only if you rotate it somewhere | | Version history | Usually a few weeks-months | Only if you keep dated folders |

The free-tier warning, said with love

Free tiers are real and useful — 15 GB covers a mountain of documents and years of compressed photos. But they change: limits shrink, promos end, prices creep. That's fine as long as the cloud is only one of your three copies. If free cloud is your only backup, you've outsourced 3-2-1 to a company whose business model is gently nudging you toward the paid tier. Use them. Don't marry them; and keep a local copy too.

Sync-folder trap, one more time

Because it's the most common way beginners get hurt: a syncing folder is a mirror. Delete the file locally and it's deleted in the cloud, usually instantly. Great for convenience, wrong for history. So your sync folder can be copy #3 (offsite) — just make sure copy #2 (a plain drive) has old versions of things in dated folders. The drive is your memory; the mirror is your convenience.

A worked decision

Question: "I have 40 GB of photos, 3 GB of documents. All free, no subscription. What do I do?"

  1. Photos → auto-upload to a free cloud photo tier if your 40 GB fits

(Google's 15 GB probably doesn't fit; consider their paid tier or treat phone-backup differently — next chapter). If it doesn't fit and you don't want to pay, that's fine: photos go to two physical drives, one of which lives at a relative's house. Still 3-2-1-ish: phone (1), drive A (2), drive B at Mom's (3).

  1. Documents → free cloud folder (3 GB fits under any free tier) plus

the weekly drive copy. Done.

Patterns to steal: small personal files go to the cloud; big files go to drives; everything ends up in at least two kinds of places.

Do this today

  1. Create a free cloud account if you have none — most people already have

one attached to their phone or email.

  1. Sync or upload your Document treasure folder into it (recipe 4 has the

app-level steps).

  1. Write on your worksheet: "cloud home = ______, what goes there = ______."
