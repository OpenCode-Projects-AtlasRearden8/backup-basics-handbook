Restore Drills: Backups Only Count If You Can Restore

Here's the uncomfortable little secret of the backup world: most "backups" are feelings, not systems. The drive blinks. The cloud icon is blue. Everything seems backed up. Nobody has ever tried getting a file back.

This chapter turns your backup from a feeling into a tested system, the way fire drills turn alarm bells into muscle memory. Every three months: one file, one restore, one verify. Fifteen minutes, tops.

The drill, universal version

  1. Pick a test file. Choose a real file from the middle of your treasure

folder — a photo, a spreadsheet, whatever. Pick a real one, not a throwaway doc; the point is confidence, and fake drills breed fake calm.

  1. Copy it to a temp spot (the "original, still safe" copy — we're not

being brave here; we're being smart).

  1. Delete the file from its home folder. Deep breath — the temp copy

means nothing can actually hurt you.

  1. Empty trash/Recycle Bin too, if you want the full-clear drill (skip

for round one — no shame).

  1. Restore it. Here's where the actual backup gets to show what it

knows:

  • Drive copy: open the drive, find the matching file in your dated

folders, drag it back.

  • Cloud/mirror: log in (a different device is a fairer test —

phone instead of computer) and download it.

  • Phone photos: open the photo app on another device and see the

photo exists there; ideally, download/save one to be sure.

  1. Verify, don't squint. Open the restored file. For anything with

two-copy trick, check the checksum/screening step from recipe 2 — on Linux/Mac that's one cksum/shasum command, as shown there.

  1. Log it. Date a line in your worksheet's drill log: "2026-10-25,

restored lease.pdf from drive A, opened fine." Three satisfied checkmarks a year beats a nagging feeling of maybe.

What the drill actually tests

This is why it works — each restore path has a different failure mode, and the drill catches the real one:

  • The setup failed silently. A backup app that hasn't actually backed

anything up since the last OS update (the classic).

  • You can't get in. Password expired, auth app lost, account locked

out. Discovering this from your couch is vastly better than discovering it from the worst possible day.

  • The restore path is missing or wrong. The file's there but no app

opens it, or the drive's dated-folders structure didn't match what your muscle memory assumed.

  • The entire drive is dead. Yes, drives die even while sitting in

drawers. Better to learn it on drill day.

Cloud-only drill, extra version (twice a year)

Log in from another device entirely, and actually download — no peeking, real download:

  1. On a different device (your phone, or a friend's computer logged into a

private window — never a shared/public computer), open the cloud folder.

  1. Download one file. Open it. It's identical? Pass.
  2. Also check the free-space and account-status pages once: limits, billing,

password freshness.

Phone-photos drill (the one with the feelings)

Twice a year, on your computer's browser:

  1. Open the photo site and search for a specific memory ("beach 2023").
  2. Download one photo.
  3. If you keep a paper/archive copy: flip through your latest dated photo

folder for a minute and confirm it looks sane.

This one drill is what stops the 3 a.m. thought — "wait, does the baby album ACTUALLY exist anywhere else?" — from ever coming back.

Keeping score on the worksheet

The worksheet has a small drill log on it. Use it like this:

| Date | What I restored | From where | Opened OK? | | --- | --- | --- | --- | | 2026-10-25 | lease PDF | drive A | yes |

A quarter with no entry means the rhythm slipped. Gently restart the rhythm; the recipes in the back make each session mechanical.

Do this today

  1. Open recipe 2 ("Prove You Can Restore") — it's the drill, command

versions included, with your first log line ready to write.

  1. Run it once. Right now. Yeah — you, today, now. It's fifteen minutes.
  2. Fill the first row of your drill log. First one's free; the second is

scheduled three months out.

Make it quarterly, not someday. Calendar it now: first Saturday of the next two quarters, name it "backup drill + coffee." The coffee is load-bearing.
