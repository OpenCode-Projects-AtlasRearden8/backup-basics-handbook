Recipe 5 — The Yearly Archive Check

Goal: once a year, prove your whole archive still opens, fix weird names, and migrate anything in formats going stale. Thirty minutes in, decades of quiet confidence out.

You need: your archive (drive, cloud, or both), your current computer, and a refill of whatever beverage keeps you at the desk.

The yearly pass

1. Open the archive itself. Browse the drive — is the folder structure readable? Cloud folder — does everything still say "synced"?

2. The open test: three files, at random.

  • One PDF — opens, renders, looks right.
  • The oldest file in the whole archive — this is the canary. If the

ten-year-old text/photo opens, your formats are aging gracefully.

  • One spreadsheet/CSV or document — opens with real data, not garbage

characters.

3. Name sweep. Scroll for a minute. Anything you can't exactly identify from its name alone gets renamed with the date pattern (YYYY-MM-DD-what-it-is.pdf). Future-you writes thank-you notes.

4. Format migrations. Move anything living on old media — CD/DVDs, camera cards, unlabeled USB sticks — into your normal folders, in modern formats (chapter 6's table). Write "migrated the old camera-card files into the archive" on the worksheet so you know it happened.

5. Location sanity. Offsite copy still offsite? (Drive B still at Aunt Rosa's? Cloud account still logged in from your phone?) Twenty seconds, and this is the check that normally catches the one physical copy that wandered into been-lost-land.

6. Version hygiene. If you keep dated backup folders (Backups-2024-11, Backups-2025-08, Backups-2026-10), keep them until the drive is nearly full, then delete the oldest only. Never delete the newest. That's the whole retention policy. Officials would call it "grandfather-father-son"; you can call it "keep a few."

Check your work, mechanically

On Linux/macOS, list your archive with sizes and sanity checks:

# what's actually in the archive, biggest things first
du -ah /media/YOURDRIVE/Backups/  | sort -rh | head -20

# every PDF and JPEG there, counted by type
find /media/YOURDRIVE/Backups/ -type f \( -iname "*.pdf" -o -iname "*.jpg" \) | wc -l

# text files that might actually be empty or corrupt (zero bytes)
find /media/YOURDRIVE/Backups/ -type f -empty

Tested: these three commands were run in this handbook's test shell against a built backup tree — they behave exactly as described.

Zero-byte surprises (-empty) are rare but real; if any appear, check their twins in a newer backup folder before panicking, and copy over the healthy version.

If something's off

  • An old file won't open → find its plain twin (PDF next to the

fancy app file; text/.md next to the rich document). Update the alive copy, re-save, and write the lesson on the worksheet.

  • Archive looks scrambled or names are hopeless → don't fix it all

today. Set a monthly 20-minute "archive tidying" block; four months of gentle renaming beats one heroic night.

  • You found files that exist nowhere else → congratulations: the

yearly check just did exactly what it's for. Copy them into the treasure folder, run recipe 1, and enjoy having dodged a small tragedy on purpose.

That's the last recipe. After it: give your worksheet a date, pour something nice, and go tell someone they should borrow this book.
